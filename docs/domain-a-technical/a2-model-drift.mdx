## Layer 1 — Start here

> AI model performance degrades over time as the real world shifts away from training conditions — silently, without visible error, until the consequences become apparent downstream.

Unlike traditional software, AI models can become less accurate over time as the world changes around them. Models trained on pre-pandemic behaviour broke when spending patterns shifted in 2020. A hosted language model can change underneath you when the vendor updates it. The model does not alert you — it continues producing outputs that are increasingly wrong.

## Layer 2 — Overview

### Risk description

AI models are trained at a point in time on data reflecting the world as it was. Three mechanisms drive degradation: data drift (input distribution changes), concept drift (the relationship the model learned no longer holds), and model decay (representations become stale without identifiable input changes). Because models rarely fail visibly when drifting, degradation can persist undetected for months.

### Likelihood drivers
- No monitoring framework in place post-deployment
- No scheduled review dates in the AI Register
- Rapidly changing external environment (inflation, economic shocks, regulatory change)
- Continuous learning systems updating from potentially shifted incoming data
- Vendor-hosted models (LLM APIs) updated or retired by the provider, often behind an unchanged model name

### Consequence types
| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| Financial loss | Underwriting model pricing stale risk relationships |
| Operational degradation | Claims triage model becoming systematically inaccurate |
| Customer harm | Fraud model false-positive spike affecting legitimate customers |
| Regulatory exposure | Model performance no longer meets documented standards |

### Affected functions
Risk · Actuarial · Credit · Fraud · Claims · Underwriting · Operations

## Layer 3 — Controls detail

### A2-001 — Continuous performance monitoring
**Owner:** Technology | **Type:** Detective | **Effort:** Medium | **Go-live required:** Yes

Track accuracy, precision, recall, F1, or domain-relevant KPIs on a rolling schedule against a held-out validation set. Dashboard reviewed at minimum monthly by the model owner.

### A2-002 — Statistical drift detection
**Owner:** Technology | **Type:** Detective | **Effort:** Medium | **Go-live required:** Yes

Implement PSI (Population Stability Index) on key input features and on the model's score distribution. A common rule of thumb treats PSI above 0.1 as worth investigating and above 0.25 as significant shift; these are conventions, not a standard, so calibrate them per model. A two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test is an alternative for continuous features. Input drift does not prove concept drift: confirming that the relationship the model learned has changed needs ground-truth outcomes, so track performance on labelled outcomes as they arrive (A2-001). Shifts in feature attributions (e.g. SHAP) are a useful early signal where labels arrive late. Alert thresholds documented in the model risk record.

For vendor-hosted LLMs, pin to a dated model version where the provider offers one, track the provider's deprecation schedule, and re-run a fixed evaluation set before any version change and on a schedule.

### A2-003 — Retraining triggers
**Owner:** Risk | **Type:** Corrective | **Effort:** Low | **Go-live required:** Yes

Define thresholds at which performance degradation triggers retraining or revalidation. Document in the model risk record. Include a scheduled revalidation trigger regardless of metric status — quarterly for models driving customer or credit decisions, at least annually otherwise.

**Jurisdiction notes:** AU — APRA CPS 230 (in force 1 July 2025) — operational risk management for critical operations, including the technology they depend on | EU — AI Act Art. 72 — providers of high-risk systems must run post-market monitoring (Annex III high-risk obligations apply from 2 December 2027 under Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 — see B2) | UK — PRA SS1/23 Model risk management principles for banks (effective 17 May 2024) — expects ongoing monitoring of model performance | US — Federal Reserve SR 11-7 — model risk guidance requires ongoing monitoring, including outcomes analysis

### KPIs
| Metric | Target | Frequency |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| PSI on primary features and score | < 0.1 (investigate 0.1–0.25) | Weekly |
| Model performance vs baseline | < 5% degradation | Monthly |

## Layer 4 — Technical implementation

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

def calculate_psi(expected, actual, buckets=10, eps=1e-6):
    """Population Stability Index between a baseline and a current sample.

    Bins are fixed from the baseline's quantiles and applied to both samples.
    Rule of thumb: < 0.1 stable, 0.1-0.25 investigate, > 0.25 significant shift.
    """
    expected, actual = np.asarray(expected), np.asarray(actual)
    edges = np.unique(np.quantile(expected, np.linspace(0, 1, buckets + 1)))
    edges[0], edges[-1] = -np.inf, np.inf          # catch values outside the baseline range
    e = np.histogram(expected, edges)[0] / len(expected)
    a = np.histogram(actual, edges)[0] / len(actual)
    e, a = np.clip(e, eps, None), np.clip(a, eps, None)  # avoid log(0) for empty bins
    return float(np.sum((a - e) * np.log(a / e)))

def ks_shift(expected, actual, alpha=0.01):
    """Two-sample Kolmogorov-Smirnov test; True if the distributions differ."""
    return stats.ks_2samp(expected, actual).pvalue < alpha
```

## Incident examples

**Models broken by pandemic behaviour (2020):** When spending and shopping patterns shifted abruptly in early 2020, models trained on pre-pandemic data — demand forecasting, inventory, fraud and recommendation systems — produced unreliable outputs, and many organisations had to intervene manually or retrain. (MIT Technology Review, "Our weird behavior during the pandemic is messing with AI models", May 2020)

**Zillow Offers wind-down (November 2021):** Zillow closed its algorithm-driven home-buying business after its pricing models failed to keep up with a shifting housing market, writing down about US$304 million of inventory in Q3 2021 and cutting about a quarter of its workforce. Its CEO cited the unpredictability of forecasting home prices. (Zillow Group Q3 2021 shareholder letter; AP and Bisnow reporting, November 2021)

**Same model name, different behaviour (2023):** Researchers compared the March and June 2023 versions of GPT-4 behind the same API and found large changes on fixed tasks; on identifying prime versus composite numbers, accuracy fell from 84% to 51%. Teams relying on a hosted model had no visibility of the change. (Chen, Zaharia and Zou, *Harvard Data Science Review*, 2024)

## Scenario seed
**Context:** A bank's fraud detection model has been in production for 18 months. No monitoring framework exists.

**Trigger:** The risk team receives an unexplained spike in customer complaints about legitimate transactions being declined.

**Complicating factor:** The model's aggregate accuracy metric (measured quarterly) has not degraded — the drift is concentrated in a specific customer segment not well-represented in the validation set.

**Discussion questions:** What monitoring would have detected this earlier? How do you investigate drift when aggregate metrics look healthy? Who is accountable for this outcome?

**Difficulty:** Intermediate | **Jurisdictions:** AU, Global
